Plum and Peach

One palette, five ideas about what color should do in Shoebox. Round one only changed the paint. These change what the color means. The last section takes the corner radius on its own, so you can see how much of the softness came from it.

Idea 1

Sharpened

The same palette with small corners (4 pixels), thin outlines instead of filled grey buttons, and plum only on the one action that matters.

Tests: whether the radius was the problem. If this already feels better, the palette was fine all along.

Idea 2

Dusk to dawn

Shoebox is about the right moment, so the color follows the time of day. Mornings are peach paper with plum, midday is pale, and evenings turn to plum with peach type.

Strength: you know when something came back by its color alone, and evening notifications don't glare. Cost: three looks to keep consistent.

Idea 3

Ripening

Color shows how long you've kept something. A fresh save is peach. The longer it waits, the deeper it gets, through rose to plum, like fruit ripening.

Strength: the "Coming back" list tells you at a glance what you've been carrying the longest. Cost: five shades to manage, and color can't be the only signal, so the age is written too.

Idea 4

Two-ink print

Plum and peach as two printing inks, like a risograph zine. Where they overlap they make a third color. A fine grain, square corners and the Daily Issue's layouts.

Strength: nothing else on a phone looks like this, and it belongs with the Daily Issue cover. Cost: it's a strong style; it has to stay out of the way on everyday screens.

Idea 5

Plum night

Dark first. Most of Shoebox now happens on the Lock Screen, which is dark most of the time. Deep plum surfaces with peach type and peach actions.

Strength: it looks native on the Lock Screen and calm at night. Cost: the light version needs equal care; Idea 1 would be its daytime twin.

The radius

How round?

The same notification at four corner sizes. The phone's own corners stay round; this is only about Shoebox's cards and buttons.

My take: 4 pixels. It reads as paper and print, not as a bubble, and it still feels like it belongs on an iPhone.
