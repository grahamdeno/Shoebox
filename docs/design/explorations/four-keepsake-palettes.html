Shoebox colors

Four palettes, each taken from a real thing people keep in a shoebox, so every color has a reason and a job. Each is painted on the same three moments from the storyboard: the morning issue, an evening notification, and Coming back.

Palette 1

Carbon Copy

The note you wrote to yourself, in triplicate. Onionskin white, carbon-paper blue for your words and the one action, and the two copy colors: canary for what's coming back, pink for what you've finished.

From: the white, yellow and pink copies of an old receipt book.

Developing

An instant photo coming into view. It starts as a murky olive grey, then warm amber and teal bloom out of it. That is exactly what Shoebox does: something you kept slowly comes back. White frame, olive night, amber to act, teal for done.

From: an instant photo in the first minutes after it comes out of the camera.

Palette 3

Cyanotype

A sun print. You lay a leaf on treated paper, leave it in the light, and time makes the image in Prussian blue. One deep blue does almost everything, with kraft for what's new and a sun-faded blue for done. The calmest and most serious of the four.

From: a sun print made on a summer afternoon, kept in an envelope.

Palette 4

Pressed Flower

A violet pressed between the pages of a book so you'd remember the day. Parchment, brown-black ink, violet to act, petal pink for what's new and the sage of the stem for done. The closest to Plum and Peach, but gentler and less expected.

From: a flower pressed in a book, found years later.
