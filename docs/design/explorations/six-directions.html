Shoebox Directions

Six ways Shoebox could look and move. Every phone shows the same saves. Each direction gets two screens: Today, and the one moment that makes it different.

Direction 1

The Stack

One thing at a time. Today is a short stack of cards, and your reason is the headline. The stack gets thinner as you go.

Clear
1 swipe right
Later
Swipe down, then a day: 2
Find old
Pull past the top, type
Build
Web works; native feels better

Gives up the whole-day view. Swiping can feel like a dating app unless the motion stays slow.

Direction 2

The Daily Issue

Each morning is a small magazine made only of what you saved. Your reasons are the cover lines. Each item gets a layout made for its kind.

Clear
1 tap at the foot of the page
Later
2
Find old
Back issues, or search
Build
Web; the layouts are the work

Gives up predictability: a day of plain links makes a plain cover. The most design work to build well.

Direction 3

The Box

The shoebox, for real. Today's few lie on the lid. Everything else is filed inside like index cards, with tabs for months and topics.

Clear
Flick into "back in the box": 1
Later
Drag to "clip to a day": 1
Find old
Lift the lid, tap a tab: 2
Build
Native, or careful web. Most expensive.

Gives up restraint. It turns into clip art without real craft, and it's the hardest to make fast and accessible.

Direction 4

The Ribbon

Coming back is about time, so time is how you get around. Finished things drift left, today sits in the middle, and set-aside things wait on their day to the right. No tabs.

Clear
1 swipe on the line
Later
Drag onto a day: 1
Find old
Pull down, type
Build
Web works well

Gives up some familiarity: sideways movement takes a minute to learn, and topics come second to time.

Recommended, with The Page's type
Direction 5

The Page

Almost nothing on screen. Today is a list on a legal pad: your reasons as sentences, in order. Done draws an ink line through it.

Clear
1 swipe: the line strikes through
Later
Hold, then a day: 2
Find old
Type on the same page
Build
Web; cheapest of the six

Gives up pictures until you open an item. A new user may read it as unfinished, and a save with no reason looks thin.

Direction 6

The Thread

A running exchange with your past self. Saving is sending yourself a note. Each morning, what you sent comes back, and you answer it.

Save
Paste, then send: 1 tap
Clear
1 tap on a reply
Later
2
Build
Web

Gives up distinctiveness unless it's crafted carefully; it can read as another chat app. The thread grows long.
